Your mouse is where you look. A line forms in front of you, facing it. Left-drag draws where the company should stand instead.

Being heard

Your voice carries ~50 m. Further off, only the drummer can beat your order down the line, two seconds late. Lose him and you must walk to your men.

Firing

B Fire by rank — the front rank fires, then the next, so some muskets are always loaded.

5 Make ready — the front two ranks present and hold. F / Space FIRE — everyone presented lets go at once. A volley close in breaks companies; at 150 m it wastes powder. Reloading takes about a minute. 6 lets each man fire when he has a mark. Men only fire when halted.

Moving

1 Follow me — a column that walks in your footsteps. 2 Form line. 3 Advance (wheels to face your mouse). 4 Halt. 9 Fall back, facing the enemy. 8 Skirmish order — loose, hard to hit, easy to ride over. 0 Column of attack — narrow and deep: it barely fires and suffers more, but its charge hits far harder. Skirmish, column of attack and fire by rank are learned at camp.

Nerve

Men fall back when shot, faster when it comes all at once or from the flank. The colours, the music and you nearby steady them. R Rally — in person — can turn a rout: get among the runners (they slow down when you are with them) and shout. If it does not take the first time, stay with them and shout again; you have about half a minute before they are gone.

The bayonet

C Charge. They go in at the double and only rush the last 40 m — order it from too far and they arrive blown. A line still presented fires on its own at 30 m, and then somebody's nerve goes — and momentum counts: how fast they are going when they land and whether they are running down onto you or up into you: a steady, loaded line stops most charges; a shaken one with empty muskets breaks. Charge when they waver. T Re-form (Bayonet → Re-form) calls a charge off and closes them up on the colours, facing the nearest enemy; 4 Halt stops them where they stand. Rally does not — Rally steadies men and turns a rout.

Cavalry & the square

When you hear the trumpet and someone shouts Cavalry!, you have a few seconds. E Form square (Form → Square): four faces, every man facing out. Once it is dressed — about eight seconds, quicker for a well-drilled company — no horse will ride onto it. But a square cannot march, fires only a quarter of its muskets each way, and every ball fired into it finds somebody. A square still forming is worse than a line. Horse rides down skirmishers, lines caught reloading or taken in the flank, and anyone running. A wood, a wall or a village is as good as a square. Chasseurs come first; lancers and then cuirassiers later in the campaign.

The guns

French batteries fire twice a minute and reach right across the field — but only at what they can see: stand behind a crest and they cannot touch you. A roundshot skips through whatever is in its path, so a line two deep loses a man or two, and a square or a column loses a file (horse and guns together are the nightmare: square for the one, slaughter from the other). Skirmishers hardly notice it. Inside 140 m they fire canister, and a company walking straight at the guns is swept away. To take them: get close under cover, come at them from the flank (a gun swings round slowly past its front), or go out in skirmish order just beyond canister range and shoot down the gunners. A gun with no gunners near it is yours if you walk onto it.

The road

Every field has a line of retreat (the green ring on your side). X Retire — about face and march for it; they cannot fire, and every hit hurts like one from the flank. Men who reach it are off the field and safe. Stand on it yourself and Leave the field (L). Anyone still out there within 150 m of the enemy is taken prisoner, colours too.
